What the instruments hear

Twenty-four hours of ground motion from one seismometer per drainage, and the summit. Each line is an hour. Earthquakes show as spikes, wind and rivers as a thicker trace. A lahar or large debris flow would show as a long, swelling band lasting many minutes, first on the stations high in the valley, then on the ones below.

Ground-motion level, last 7 days

RSAM (real-time seismic amplitude): the average shaking every 10 minutes, filtered to 1–10 Hz, the band where volcanic tremor and debris flows show up. It is in raw instrument counts, so compare a station with itself, not with its neighbours. Daily cycles are mostly wind, traffic and meltwater.

Earthquakes by depth, last 30 days

Every located earthquake within 20 km of the summit: when, how deep, and how big. Most at Rainier are shallow, a few kilometres beneath the summit, in the hydrothermal system above the magma.
